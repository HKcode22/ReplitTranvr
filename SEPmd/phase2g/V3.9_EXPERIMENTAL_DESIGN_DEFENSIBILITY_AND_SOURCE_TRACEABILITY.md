# 1. Evidence hierarchy used by this project

Every important V3.9 rule should answer six questions:

1. **Construct:** what scientific quantity or behavior are we trying to measure?
2. **External basis:** what peer-reviewed, statistical, government, or provider source supports that construct?
3. **Operationalization:** what exact project rule turns that construct into a measurable experiment?
4. **Implementation:** what code/schema enforces or calculates it?
5. **Verification:** what test/gate proves the implementation matches the rule?
6. **Limitation:** what part is project-specific, uncertain, or not directly established by the literature?

The strongest traceability chain is:


# 2. External research and factual source foundation

## 2.1 Chen & Li 2019 — chained flight-delay prediction

**Jun Chen, San Diego State University; Meng Li, Purdue University.  
“Chained Predictions of Flight Delay Using Machine Learning.” AIAA SciTech 2019.**

Source:
https://junchen.sdsu.edu/proceedings/scitech_gnc19_Chen.pdf

The paper:
- combines BTS airline on-time data;
- NOAA Local Climatological Data;
- FAA ASPM airport capacity/throughput data;
- studies ORD-related flights from July 2016 to June 2017;
- uses 30 ORD-related airports;
- bins delay in 15-minute groups;
- uses random forest classifiers;
- uses SMOTE for class imbalance;
- uses recursive feature elimination with five-fold cross-validation;
- explicitly models Late Arriving Aircraft Delay;
- builds a chained prediction model along the **same aircraft's itinerary**.

The paper states that departure delay and late-arriving-aircraft delay are particularly important features and demonstrates chained prediction along the same aircraft's scheduled itinerary.

### V3.9 implications supported
- previous-flight delay is scientifically meaningful;
- same-aircraft/tail continuity matters;
- flight-leg order matters;
- weather, schedule and airport operational context are plausible predictors;
- model complexity should be evaluated rather than assumed.

### What this paper does NOT prescribe
- our six airports;
- our 2-hour Stage-1 window;
- our 4-hour Stage-2 concept;
- 500 provider credits;
- 60 rows/hour;
- WSSS as a reference;
- our exact anchor-score weights.

## 2.2 Zheng, Wei & Hu 2021 — SJSU-affiliated delay propagation

**Zhe Zheng, Wenbin Wei, Minghua Hu.  
“A Comparative Analysis of Delay Propagation on Departure and Arrival Flights for a Chinese Case Study.” Aerospace 8(8):212, 2021.**

Sources:
https://www.mdpi.com/2226-4310/8/8/212  
https://scholarworks.sjsu.edu/faculty_rsca/2410/

Wenbin Wei is affiliated with the Department of Aviation and Technology at San José State University.

The study analyzes delay propagation using operation-, time-, and weather-related variables. It explicitly studies previous delay, buffer effects and aircraft utilization. The paper discusses:
- previous arrival/departure delay;
- turnaround and block buffer;
- airport congestion;
- weather;
- aircraft type;
- flight order within the aircraft's day;
- number of flights operated by an aircraft.

### V3.9 implications supported
- delay propagation requires knowing which leg preceded the current leg;
- aircraft/tail identity is scientifically meaningful;
- later legs can experience different propagation behavior;
- operational and weather context should be represented;
- one service label alone is insufficient for aircraft-chain reasoning.

## 2.3 Zheng, Zou, Wei & Tian 2023 — SJSU-affiliated airborne ETA

**Zhe Zheng, Bo Zou, Wenbin Wei, Wen Tian.  
“A Data-Light and Trajectory-Based Machine Learning Approach for the Online Prediction of Flight Time of Arrival.” Aerospace 10(8):675, 2023.**

Sources:
https://www.mdpi.com/2226-4310/10/8/675  
https://scholarworks.sjsu.edu/faculty_rsca/4774/

The study:
- uses real flight trajectory records;
- reconstructs irregularly spaced trajectory points;
- identifies similar historical trajectories;
- predicts remaining trajectory using LSTM;
- predicts speed using gradient boosting;
- combines predicted trajectory/speed for ETA;
- explicitly separates offline training from online airborne prediction.

### V3.9 implications supported
- AIRBORNE prediction is a distinct information state from PRE-DEPARTURE;
- preserving trajectory history is scientifically useful;
- sequence/time ordering matters;
- model evaluation should reflect the information actually available online.

## 2.4 NIST/SEMATECH Engineering Statistics Handbook

Primary source:
https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/nistsematech-engineering-statistics-handbook

Relevant topics:
- populations and samples;
- sampling plans;
- stratification;
- sample-size/precision/cost tradeoffs;
- blocking nuisance factors;
- repeatability/reproducibility;
- measurement-system stability;
- design of experiments;
- variance and uncertainty.

Useful sections:
- sampling/populations:
  https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/ppc/section1/ppc134.htm
- define a sampling plan:
  https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/ppc/section3/ppc33.htm
- sampling schemes/stratification:
  https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/ppc/section3/ppc332.htm
- sample-size considerations:
  https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/ppc/section3/ppc333.htm
- blocking nuisance factors:
  https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pri/section3/pri332.htm

NIST explicitly notes that sample-size choice depends on:
- the parameter being estimated;
- sampling cost;
- prior information;
- variability;
- practicality;
- desired precision.

NIST also explains that blocking can control important nuisance factors such as time of day.

### V3.9 implications supported
- sampling design should be planned before collecting outcomes;
- population and observed sample must not be conflated;
- stratification is a defensible way to preserve coverage across heterogeneous contexts;
- time/day can be treated as nuisance/context factors;
- sample size is a precision/cost decision, not a universal constant.

### What NIST does NOT prescribe
NIST does not tell this project:
- use exactly six airports;
- use exactly 120 minutes;
- use exactly 500 credits;
- use exactly 15-minute buckets.

Those are project operationalizations.

- documentation/specification truth;
- implementation/code truth;
- live/runtime truth.

It also prioritizes P0/P1/P2/P3 issues.

A computational experiment can fail at multiple layers:
- correct scientific idea, wrong code;
- correct code, wrong deployment;
- correct deployment, incomplete data;
- correct transport, wrong measurement definition.

P2G13 is a direct example: provider execution succeeded while a scientific identity calculation was invalid.

## Source class
Research governance + measurement-system engineering.

Exact P0/P1/P2/P3 labels.

# 4. Plan §2 — locked end-to-end architecture

## Design
Separate:
- provider-observable population;
- raw events;
- clean semantic observations;
- current state;

Population vs sample; measurement provenance; temporal validity.

# 9. Plan §6 — PRE versus AIRBORNE state

## Design
Maintain separate prediction states:
- PRE-DEPARTURE;
- AIRBORNE/POST.

## Research basis
Zheng et al. 2023 directly supports online airborne ETA prediction from trajectory state.

## Why
Information available before departure is fundamentally different from information available after takeoff.

## Risk prevented
Temporal leakage.

# 10. Plan §6 timestamp taxonomy

## Design
Track distinct clocks:
- scheduled physical/event time;
- provider/source time;
- retrieval time;
- application available_at;
- prediction cutoff;
- schedule revision time.

## Why defensible
A prediction can only use information available at its cutoff.

Example:
A callback received at 12:02 may describe a flight scheduled for 11:00. Those timestamps mean different things.

## Scientific principle
As-known-at-time prediction / causal chronology.

# 11. Plan §7.1 — canonical flight_instance_id

## Construct
One actual scheduled operating leg = one physical flight instance.

## Why flight_number alone is insufficient
A flight number is a service label that can:
- repeat on different dates;
- be operated by different tails;
- participate in codeshares.

## Research basis
Aircraft-chain studies require:
- previous flight;
- tail number;
- flight order;
- itinerary sequence.

## Legacy implementation mismatch
Historical WSSS/OMAA Stage-1 metric code used proxies including:
~~~sql
COUNT(DISTINCT flight_number)
~~~

That was less precise than the Plan's physical-leg construct.

## Current correction
Physical-v2 uses stable physical identity and explicit ambiguity handling.

# 45. Stage-1 failure history as scientific learning

The repeated WSSS/MMUN failures are not evidence that the experimental method is useless.

They exposed distinct measurement-system defects:
- runtime durability;
- accounting completeness;
- provider control-plane behavior;
- state-machine censoring;
- secret binding;
- metric identity semantics.

The scientifically correct response is:
1. preserve failed evidence;
2. identify root cause;
3. fix the mechanism;
4. add regression protection;
5. rerun only when the previous measurement was invalid for a non-outcome reason.

# 46. Why WSSS/OMAA v2 correction is not outcome shopping

Historical WSSS and OMAA provider runs succeeded.

Their remeasurement is required because:
1. common ranking uses physical-flight yield;
2. legacy metrics used flight-number/runtime-key proxies;
3. future candidates use physical-v2;
4. §9.2 uses WSSS/OMAA as reference values;
5. the constructs must match;
6. old raw detailed rows are gone;
7. therefore comparable measurement must be recollected if the current score is retained.

The reason exists independently of their observed historical scores.

To prevent outcome shopping:
- the recovery order is frozen before new v2 outcomes;
- each gets one v2 correction attempt;
- no automatic retry.

# 49. How to defend the project to a teammate or professor

A precise explanation:

> “The project is not copying one professor's experiment. It synthesizes aviation-delay research showing the importance of previous-flight delay, same-aircraft propagation, weather, operational context and airborne trajectory state. It uses established statistical principles such as stratification, nuisance-factor control, sample-size/cost/precision tradeoffs, and measurement reproducibility. We then prospectively operationalized those principles for AeroDataBox under a finite credit budget. Exact constants such as a two-hour Stage-1 window are project protocol decisions, not falsely attributed to the papers. Each major rule is mapped to code, tests, exact runtime hashes and run evidence.”

Do not claim:
- SJSU proved two hours is optimal;
- NIST selected these six airports;
- a professor specified our exact credit cap;
- AI authority alone makes the protocol valid.

# 53. Final rule

When defending any individual design choice, do not ask only:

> “Is there a paper about something similar?”

Ask:

> “What construct are we measuring, what external evidence supports that construct, why did we operationalize it this way, what alternatives exist, what limitation remains, and what evidence proves the code actually executed the frozen rule?”

That is the standard this project should use before claiming scientific validity.
