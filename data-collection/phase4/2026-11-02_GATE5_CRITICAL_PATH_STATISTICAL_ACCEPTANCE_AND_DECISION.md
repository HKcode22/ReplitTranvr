## No such thing as a statistically valid automatic PASS from tolerant missingness

**Different uncertainties require different responses:**

1. **Transient ≤3-minute missed deliveries/telemetry**: may be accepted under original scientific acceptance protocol **when independently measured**; report clocks, window length, what was missed and label censoring. A permissible short gap is not a license to invent actual timestamps, retroactively fill pre-cutoff data or assume every flight is present.
2. **Missing but well-defined outcome**: identify the set of possible binary risks without assuming missing-at-random. For `A` *verified known-applicable physical flights*, `k` per-flight independently valid labeled outcomes, and `x` positives, the sharp worst-case sample-frequency bounds under arbitrary missingness are **[x/A, (x + A − k)/A]**, for `A > 0`; width `(A−k)/A`. Example: 100 genuinely applicable, 90 verified labels, 10 positives → risk between **10% and 20%**; naive observed-only estimate `10/90=11.1%` is potentially selection-biased. If `k=0`, bounds **[0%,100%]**; no useful primary label conclusion. These bounds are descriptive finite-cohort identification intervals, **not 95% confidence intervals**.
3. **Unknown target applicability or physical flight denominator**: report rates **NULL** and a separate unresolved category. The known-applicable bound cannot be generalized to all observed flights when applicability is unknown. Distinct FIDS refreshes and marketing codeshares cannot be counted as independent operating flights. For `A=0`, risk is undefined rather than 0%.
4. **Missing provider-native meaning**: a value with no verified scheduled T or actual-only target definition is **structurally unidentified**. Even 1,000,000 source rows or a 0.1% missingness rate would not prove that a generic timestamp measures wheels-off. Treat alternate status/ADSB proxy as a **different target** with independently versioned protocol, not “minor tolerated error.”
