## 1. The conclusion, without a new perfection loop

- **Yes, current all-or-nothing acceptance of only a direct exact provider actual wheels-off could be unnecessarily conservative.** ADS-B position-history algorithms CAN produce useful estimated takeoff times for positively matched aircraft with enough low-level coverage.
- **No, an estimated or first-visible-airborne timestamp is not automatically the physical actual event.** It is a noisy measurement with missingness, interval uncertainty, identity and airport/taxi/gate-vs-runway differences. Using it as if it were a certified actual event risks target misclassification and spurious accuracy.
- **Best practical scientific rule:** freeze a minimal **evidence-tiered label registry** (true observed actual, validated ADS-B proxy, unresolved estimate/UNKNOWN) *before* prospective outcome review. Keep eligible legitimate PRE rows even when optional physical label is absent, but do not turn UNKNOWN into a zero delay or an on-time flight. Predeclare ONE primary endpoint and protected 20/5/6 split for the first study, while collecting separately labeled secondary/diagnostic endpoints when allowed, without extra unbounded GETs.
- **Do not make completing 31 prospective days a Phase5 task.** Phase5 needs correct source/identity/rights/budget/metric/quality rules and unattended code; Phase6 gathers the actual evidence; Phase7 fits/evaluates models. It is okay that the real-data ADS-B error for future specific flights is currently UNKNOWN—do not invent an arbitrary ±1 minute guarantee now.

### How model-label error causes trouble, without pretending it always destroys a model

Let true matched wheels-off be `T_actual` and chosen proxy `T_proxy`. The measurement error is `e = T_proxy - T_actual` seconds. An error of 2–3 minutes may not dominate a 90-minute severe-delay event, but **can flip a 15-minute classification near its threshold**: if the true lateness is 14 minutes and the proxy adds 3, it falsely becomes 17. Conversely negative errors can conceal a positive. Errors may be **systematic** at certain airports, runway geometries, receiver densities, aircraft types, taxi regimes, regions or storms, creating source-dependent label bias, not merely random Gaussian noise.

**Critical physical-milestone difference:** BTS and the cited 2026 operational paper define the common "departure delay" as **actual gate departure/off-block minus scheduled gate departure**. A wheels-off timestamp is later than gate-out by taxi-out and runway queueing; simply subtracting scheduled gate departure from actual wheels-off measures *schedule-to-wheels-off lateness including taxi time*, not the same gate-delay target. If a reliable scheduled runway reference exists, define that separately. NEVER silently combine gate-out with wheels-off, nor mix ETA/arrival gates with wheels-on.

## 3. What an ADSB.lol fallback could and could NOT do

**Prospective use after relevant approved GO:** an original ADB flight/service record identifies a candidate operating leg. If a specific aircraft ICAO24 hex/callsign/registration is genuinely attributable to the **same physical leg and scheduled airport/date**, fetch only a narrow authorized trace (live as-of receiver observations and/or next-day historical per-aircraft archive), preserve evidence/source/observed/available timestamps and license. Review descending/ascending time, near-airport geography, altitude relative to airport, groundspeed/vertical climb, ground state and quality. Segment repeated callsigns/tails and require compatible operating airline, route, times, destination and no identity conflict. Link to an actual operator/airport or delayed BTS reference when eligible to validate empirical error.

- With a trustworthy last ground observation at `g` and first airborne/climbing observation at `a`, form a **candidate transition bracket** `[g,a]`; the midpoint or a preregistered near-runway algorithm may be an **estimated** takeoff. If no ground observation, only first-air observation is a loose upper timestamp, **not wheels-off**. Even a narrow bracket is not a mathematical guarantee if receiver age, ground bit, location or identity is wrong.
- If a unique flight link cannot be established, keep the aircraft trace **unjoined** and physical outcome UNKNOWN. Don't join Oct29 ADS-B planes to Nov6 FIDS services, let an observed aircraft promote a marketing-only source identity, or claim this solves all regional receiver gaps.
- If the actual reference is unavailable, source trace may still yield **B-tier proxy** for separately declared secondary/sensitivity research, but **its absolute error is UNKNOWN**. Cross-source agreement can strengthen confidence, but common ADS-B-derived inputs may have correlated errors, not genuinely independent ground truth.
- Missing optional ADSB/NOAA must never invalidate a truly cutoff-safe original FIDS PRE. The ADS-B/physical event is **POST outcome information** and cannot be inserted into T−24h/T−6h/T−90m features retroactively; a separate AIRBORNE prediction state may use positions available before *that* airborne cutoff.

## 4. A finite validation design — no arbitrary exact-second rule

**Predeclare sample/matching first**, never select only successful/easy plane tracks after seeing their errors. Where permission and reference availability permit, use a *precommitted* bounded stratified subset by HUB/MID/REGIONAL, airport, receiver coverage, carrier/aircraft/route and provider status/estimated-vs-actual indication. Record every eligible denominator, even no-coverage and unmatched/ambiguous. Use the original held-out 20 TRAIN / 5 VALIDATION / 6 TEST by batch-day: calibrate any proxy admission threshold on separately legal historical references or TRAIN/VALIDATION **only**, not future TEST outcomes.

For each validated **same-operating-leg, same-milestone** pair: signed takeoff proxy bias; absolute error median, mean and p90; fraction within **1, 5, 10 minutes**; `g→a` observed interval width; positive matched fraction among every eligible airport/source/tier; coverage across weather/receiver site/route; independent actual reference source and legitimacy; calibration uncertainty and false classification flips around predeclared 15/60-minute thresholds. Report **per-source/per-airport** breakdown; do not extrapolate one good hub to unmeasured airports. If no reference, report `UNVERIFIED`, never a fabricated error=0.

These 1/5/10-minute values are **reporting bins, not automatic gate requirements**. If a proxy is accurate enough for a particular task, a future independent reviewer can preregister its use and sensitivity bounds, with separate `observed_actual`, `validated_proxy`, `unverified_estimate` label tiers. Leave the first-cycle actual-only primary claim confined to actual-only evidence until a prospective amendment *explicitly* changes its definition.
