## 1. Source of the idea: published research versus our own experiment

The published research supports evaluating the *same flight at multiple lead times*, not that our exact (24h, 6h, 90m) triplet is some universal aviation requirement.

- [EUROCONTROL A-CDM Specification (2025)](https://www.eurocontrol.int/publication/eurocontrol-specification-airport-collaborative-decision-making-cdm): airport departure processes use successive operational milestones and new estimates as aircraft turnarounds and predeparture events unfold. A-CDM estimates are shared and updated; it does **not** mandate our exact three snapshots.
- [Prediction of flight departure delays caused by weather conditions adopting data-driven approaches (2023)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40537-023-00867-5): compares *six* research lead times **2, 4, 8, 16, 24 and 48 hours**, with multiple airports/long historical data. Supports a five-horizon **hypothesis**, not transporting its achieved performance, provider format, training sample size or label into our own experiment.
- [Beltman et al., Dynamically forecasting airline departure delay probability distributions for individual flights (2025)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jaitra/v126y2025ics0969699725000511.html): evaluates distribution forecasts at a **90-minute** horizon, emphasizing uncertainty and operational prediction. Does not prove an AeroDataBox 90-minute actual gate-out label is always available.
- [Bouayaben & Fricke, Real-Time Event-Driven Prediction of Departure Delay Distributions for Operational Support in Airline Networks (2026)](https://fis.tu-dresden.de/portal/en/publications/realtime-eventdriven-prediction-of-departure-delay-distributions-for-operational-support-in-airline-networks%28c6293527-f17e-4cbb-a4fe-a8ead35f4657%29.html): illustrates updating probabilistic departure-delay estimates as genuine operational events and other conditions change. Does not validate our particular subscription, accuracy or use rights.
- [FAA planning weather window](https://www.faa.gov/nextgen/programs/weather/faq) explains some disruption-planning decisions require multi-hour lead times; this is operational context, **not** a prescription for T−6h.

**Provenance conclusion:** the f.8 repo froze T−24h/T−6h/T−90m as an internal choice: early comparison, substantial lead-time principal forecast, near-departure update. There is no documented claim that a single academic study originally invented or prescribed **this exact triplet**. We should not fabricate such a citation. Other choices are legitimate hypotheses and must be compared empirically with as-of feature coverage and costs.

## 2. The predicted quantity is a flight outcome, not one FIDS record

Suppose originally **scheduled gate-out T = 14:00**, actual witnessed gate-out = 14:42:
- T−24h forecast the prior day at 14:00;
- T−6h at 08:00;
- T−90m at 12:30.
- All three predictions can be measured against **the same later outcome +42 minutes**, if both actual and original scheduled **gate-out** are independently verified for the same operating leg. T−6h as **primary prediction horizon** is not a separate physical outcome or a promise to train on only one 08:00 record.
- Later features cannot modify an older PRE. A T−90m update uses only observations actually available by its own issue time. Flight operation versus cancellation is a separate event; missing physical label is NULL/UNKNOWN, not 0.

**Distinguish milestones:** off-block/gate-out is aircraft leaving the stand; wheels-off is actual runway lift-off. Earlier [Gate 0.5 exact-runway assessor](../../server/lib/disruption/gate05RunwayDelayLabelAudit_v39.ts) compares **scheduled runway wheels-off** against **actual wheels-off**. [New Amendment E candidate](../../server/lib/disruption/phase5AmendmentEGateOutDelayLabel_v39.ts) compares **scheduled gate-out** against **actual gate-out**. Neither an ADSB.lol inferred takeoff proxy nor an AeroDataBox general scheduledTime/revisedTime automatically proves physical gate-out. [FAA ASPM variable definitions](https://www.aspm.faa.gov/aspmhelp/index/ASPM__Individual_Flights__Definitions_of_Variables.html) distinguishes gate- and runway-time variables.

**Important current code limitation:** [existing Stage14 writer](../../server/lib/disruption/phase5V0079SingleBoundFlightPreWriter_v39.ts) normally persists `features:[]` in its GH workflow unless a separately authorized and independently source-licensed route feature path is enabled. The test-only and separately gated [Amendment E bound route bridge](../../server/lib/disruption/phase5AmendmentEStage14BoundRouteFeatures_v39.ts) currently supplies at most origin/destination categorical fields; [as-of feature selector](../../server/lib/disruption/phase5AmendmentEAsOfFeatureSelectionOffline_v39.ts) is pure and recognizes candidate current FIDS status/revisions, weather and historical route/turnaround, but they are **not proven production-wired**. More empty snapshots do **not** repair missing predictive features. Prioritize actual source/right-safe FIDS revised/status, NOAA METAR/TAF, route/carrier history, congestion, previous operating leg/tail where matched, and source-lineage/availability times. Use existing [weather-as-of](../../server/lib/disruption/phase4Gate5WeatherAsOfFeature_v39.ts) and [historical as-of feature store](../../server/lib/disruption/historicalFeatureStore_v3.ts) rather than inventing a second data lake.

## 3. Could we afford five horizons? A nonpaid what-if ONLY

**Research candidate, not adopted:** add T−12h and T−3h to existing T−24h / T−6h / T−90m. These offer one middle early-warning point and one closer operational update. Actual incremental prediction information is unknown until source coverage and feature clocks are measured. Additional horizons increase same-flight correlated rows; they do **not** create extra independent operating flights.

The existing [independent Phase 6 31-day budget audit](2026-11-10_INDEPENDENT_PHASE6_PARALLEL_AUTOMATION_DESIGN_AND_BUDGET_AUDIT.md) describes **31 parent batch-days**, **34 active segments**, **four selected airport slots per day** and **three planning horizons**. That is 34×4×3 = **408 airport/segment/horizon LOGICAL planning groups**. Five horizons would make **680 groups**, 272 more. **Groups are not paid HTTP requests.** A single airport FIDS GET can return dozens of flights; other groups may need more than one request because of time coverage, pagination/partitions, refreshes, latency and signed source staleness.

**Illustrative *not verified* expense**: if every new logical group required one fresh Tier-2 FIDS physical GET, two additional horizons would mean 272 GETs ×2 units = **544 extra REST API units**. At four actual GETs per new group, it would be 1,088 GETs and 2,176 extra units. These are examples, *not a provider quote or a recommended request cadence*. The frozen historical project REST ceiling is **50,000** units per cycle, and the current real account/budget, paid POST/outcome, seven categories, retries, refills and billing-day reservations must be subtracted. Separate Flight Alert **SEND credits must NEVER be added as though they were REST units**; extra horizons alone do not necessarily create more subscriptions or SENDs.

Code: [nonpaid optional-horizon sensitivity](../../server/lib/disruption/phase5OptionalHorizonSensitivity_v39.mjs) + [tests](../../tests/phase5_optional_five_horizon_cost_sensitivity_v39.test.ts). It takes explicitly assumed **physical GET counts per proposed extra horizon**, Tier-2 cost per GET, reserved baseline and other REST units, explicitly reserved refill REST units and separate Alert balances. It shows hypothetical marginal REST and an Alert shortfall **independently**; returns `phase6PaidGo:false`, `currentThreeHorizonPlanModified:false`, `fiveHorizonPredictiveValueMeasuredByThisFunction:false`. It cannot infer request counts from 408/680 planning groups. Only independent later live provider/account receipts can turn illustrative quantities into supported exposure.

**Scientific practical decision:** retain three live horizons for Phase 6 **for now**, and log T−12h / T−3h as prospective optional research. Before making five live, require (a) actual meaningful as-of feature/label availability at the candidate issue times, (b) measured marginal physical GET/REST/Alert cost and latency, (c) evidence that adds information beyond three times and baseline, (d) future additive reviewed SQL/workflow versions, a new independently frozen 31-day source/row budget and strict horizon claim isolation, not alteration/replay of installed V0087–V0092. The repo's V0087 table CHECK, V0091 registrar, Stage14 `PRE_HORIZON_OFFSET_MIN`, V0092 claim and multiple GH roster parsers currently accept only the **three** horizon names. Merely writing new packet names would break or bypass reviewed constraints. Do not change what is frozen just to collect more rows.

## 4. Phase 7: train, test on unseen flights/days, serve a future prediction

**Phase 6**: collect real prospective *as-known-at-decision* examples from selected airports: operating service/airline, route, original and revised schedule, available weather, airport congestion, prior route/tail observations, eligible FIDS updates and separately priced Alert/webhook messages. After the actual flight, collect POST, operation/cancel/diversion and matched actual milestone outcomes with honest missingness.

**Phase 7 training**: construct one or more model datasets from earlier forecast snapshots. Possible strategies: (A) one separately trained model per horizon; (B) one time-aware model with an explicit horizon and available-information age indicators. Test both against simple status quo/current FIDS revised time, airline/route historical median and other comparable baselines. Group all observations for the **same flight into the same split**; existing provisional 20 train, 5 validation, 6 protected test **parent batch-day** design and matched crossover-pair handling are the starting point. Do not move future outcomes into earlier features. Evaluate minutes MAE/quantile pinball, calibrated interval coverage and threshold Brier scores; report feature and physical-label coverage by airport/route/airline and experiment selection; one month may be weak for rare delays, so don't promise strong generalization.

**Future inference**: for a new flight at its T−6h time, obtain *current* permitted FIDS/Alert/weather/etc. information **as of that timestamp**, transform into the same feature definitions used in training, then apply the fitted model. At T−90m re-fetch or ingest genuinely newer permitted updates and issue a new forecast; no retraining necessary per flight. **The original 4h airport treatment/probe study is NOT rerun for each new user prediction.** Live inference still needs appropriately authorized, budgeted current source access and a staleness/unknown policy; the learned model alone cannot see live flights. An after-takeoff AIRBORNE ETA uses a different data state and matched free ADSB.lol information as source terms allow.

**Takeaway:** fixing sparse/empty feature columns and true same-event labels matters **more** than increasing from three to five forecast times.
