## 2. Binding Plan conflict — source semantics

`V3.9_DataCollectPlan_f.8.md` §4.1 allows the traffic-tier metric itself to be MEASURE→FREEZE from a permitted reference, but §4.5 explicitly requires the balancing variables **network degree, carrier diversity, and international/domestic mix** to come from a permitted/licensed **scheduled-route / schedule reference**.

The current frame builder consumes one reference artifact for both the traffic tier and the §4.5 balancing variables. The MrAirspace candidate is observational: it reconstructs flights/routes from ADS-B data. It is therefore **not currently admissible as the single frame reference under unmodified V3.9-f.8**, regardless of its successful build, license, coverage, or internal validation.

The repository enforces this now. `trafficReferencePlanContract_v39.ts` requires `reference_semantics='scheduled_commercial_service'` for the current single-reference frame design. The pinned MrAirspace candidate declares `observed_commercial_movements`, so `v39:traffic:materialize`, `v39:frame:build`, and the reference/preprobe freeze chain remain blocked.

### Proposed observed-reference definitions if the Plan is formally revised

These definitions are **proposal only** until the Plan itself adopts them:

- traffic metric: `observed_commercial_movements`
- network degree: distinct observed resolved origin/destination connections with at least 53 movements in the fixed 365-day reference
- carrier diversity: upstream three-letter `Airline` ICAO operator field only; callsign prefix is diagnostic and is not substituted when `Airline` is missing
- international share: observed resolved departures where origin country differs from destination country / observed resolved departures with known country pair.

The candidate would remain only an exogenous sampling/balancing reference. It would not become the Phase-6 population denominator and would not redefine the primary FIDS passenger-flight population.

## 8. Scientific basis and boundary of the proposed substitution

Observed ADS-B data are scientifically useful for operational traffic and network measurement, but that fact does not make them identical to published schedule data. Current literature uses ADS-B/OpenSky operational data to derive airport operational milestones and to measure airport arrival/departure flows; airport-network research also commonly uses degree/centrality and observed/weighted flight flows to characterize hubs and connectivity. This supports the **plausibility** of an observed-movement exogenous reference, not an automatic equivalence to scheduled service.

Relevant examples reviewed during this audit include:

- Schultz et al., *Journal of Air Transport Management* (2022), “Data-driven airport management enabled by operational milestones derived from ADS-B messages” — ADS-B messages used to derive airport operational milestones and performance information.
- Chao (2026), *Journal of Advanced Transportation*, “Opening Low-Altitude Corridors Near Major Hubs…” — OpenSky historical flight-list records are used to count airport arrivals/departures and operational traffic.
- Cheung, Wong & Zhang, *Transportation Research Part E* (2020), “The evolution of aviation network: Global airport connectivity index 2006–2016” — degree and volumetric/network measures are used to characterize airport connectivity/hub importance.
- MrAirspace `aircraft-flight-schedules` documentation — global quarterly route/flight data are explicitly derived from ADS-B observations rather than a published airline timetable.

Therefore a formal Plan revision could reasonably define an **observed operational network reference** as a different, explicit measurement construct. It must not continue using words such as “scheduled departures” for observed quantities, and the change must be recorded before any frame/probe result can influence the decision.
